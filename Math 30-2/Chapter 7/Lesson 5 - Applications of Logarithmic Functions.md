Logarithmic functions are the inverse of exponential functions. Because of this we can change forms between exponential and logarithmic functions:$$y=a^x <=> \log_{a}y$$
Therefore: A logarithm is equal to an exponent. The expression $\log_{a}y$ means "the exponent must be applied to the base $a$ to give the value of $y$"

New: 
● The fact that a logarithm is an exponent makes logarithms very useful for certain everyday-life situations where values vary greatly. 
● A logarithmic scale with powers of 10 can be used to make comparisons between large and small values easier. 
● Three examples of logarithmic scales are the 
o Richter scale, used to measure the magnitude of earthquakes; 
o pH scale, used to measure the acidity of solutions; 
o Decibel scale, used to measure sound levels.

# Earthquakes
1. The magnitude of an earthquake, M, can  be calculated using the log function $$M=\log I$$
where I is the intensity of the earthquake measured by a seismograph

| Amplitude of vibrations on seismograph (I) | 1<br>10^0                     | 10<br>10^1      | 100<br>10^2 | 1000<br>10^3 | 10000<br>10^4 | 100000<br>10^5 | 1000000<br>10^6 |     |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- | --------------- | ----------- | ------------ | ------------- | -------------- | --------------- | --- |
| Richter Scale magnitude (M)                | log10^0<br>0log10<br>0(I) = 0 | $\log 10^1$<br> | $\log 10^2$ | $\log10^3$   | $\log 10^4$   | $\log 10^5$    | $\log 10^6$     |     |
from this we see that in comparison to an earthquake with a magnitude of 4, an earthquake with a magnitude of 5 is actually **10** times more intense.

In comparison to an earthquake with a magnitude of 2, an earthquake with a magnitude of 5 is actually 1000 times more intense

When we write the function $M=\log I$ in exponential form, it is equivalent to ____________ $10^M = I$.

Therefore, two earthquakes with a difference of x on the Richter scale have an intensity difference of $10^x$.

The logarithmic Richter scale makes it easier to compare earthquakes because it uses numbers which are much more manageable.

2. An earthquake measured 7.6 on the Richter scale. Its aftershock measured 4.1 on the Richter scale. How many more times intense was the earthquake compared to the aftershock?
$\frac{10^{7.6}}{10^{4.1}}=3162.28$ 

4. An earthquake in Cusco was measured to be 102 times more intense than an earthquake in Railay. If the magnitude of the earthquake in Cusco was 5.8 on the Richter scale, what was the magnitude of the earthquake in Railay, to the nearest tenth?
$102=\frac{10^{5.8}}{10^x}$
solve for x
$10^x=\frac{10^{5.8}}{102}$
$10^x=6185.9$
$M=\log(6185.8)$
$M=3.8$
