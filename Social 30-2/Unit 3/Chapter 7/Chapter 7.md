
# Definitions
## Bolsheviks
members of a wing of the Russian Social-democratic workers' party, led by Vladimir Lenin, which took control in the Russian Revolution of 1918 and which became the communist party of the soviet union

## Communism 
an ideology based on the belief that the oppressed working class must overcome its property-owning oppressors through revolution, so that together they can work towards becoming a classless society in which property is owned by the community and all people share in the production of goods and in the benefits of production (at which point, in theory, no government is needed)

## Dictatorship
a form of government in which one person or a small group possesses absolute power. Dictators, who are not accountale to the citizens over whome they rule, often use force or faud to gain political power, which they maintain through intimidation, terror, the repression of civil liberties and propaganda

## Dissent
to have or express an opinion different from a prevailing or official position; disagree

## Eugenics 
an ideology based on the premise that all human physical and mental characteristics are passed on through the biological heredity. It proposes that breeding be controlled to increase the occurrence of human traits considered to be desirable

## Fascism 
an authoritarian system of government (a dictatorship) that includes extreme militaristic nationalism, a belief in the rule of elites, and a totaliarian socirty which individual interests are subordinated to the good of the nation-state. Fascism rejects the ideas of both liberal democracy and communism or socialism

## Leninism
political ideology developed by russian marxist revolutionary Vladimir Lenin that proposes the establishment of the dictatorship of the proletariat led by a revolutionary vanguard party as the political prelude to the establishment of communism. 
  
## Nazism 
a fascist political ideology promoted by Adolf Hitler, based
on a complete rejection of the political values of liberalism by
creating a totalitarian dictatorship focused on the ideas of
national power and racial superiority; also called National
Socialism
  
## Social Darwinism 
an ideology which promotes the notion that
some individuals or groups achieve power and advantage over
others through competition because they are stronger and
fitter
  
## Stalinism
a political ideology based on Soviet dictator Joseph
Stalin’s interpretation of communism, characterized by
totalitarian rule and repression of political opponents
(dissidents)
  
## Subsistence Agriculture
subsistence agriculture occurs when farmers grow crops on smallholdings to meet the needs of themselves and their families. Subsistence agriculturalists target farm output for survival and mostly for local requirements. Planting decisions occur pincipally with an eye toward what the family will need during the coming year, and only secondarily toward market prices.

## Techniques of Dictatorship:
### propaganda
the systematic attempt to manipulate public opinions and beliefs, often through the mass media. All media such as television, radio, movies, books, newspapers, and even billboards are controlled by the governments
### indoctrination
Schools and other organisations are used to indoctrinate children with the beliefs of the elite. Often young people are organised into nationalist youth groups to promote the ideas of the dictator

### use of force and terror
Most dictators use force and terror to destroy all opposition to their government. Usually there is a secret police that rounds up citizens identified as enemies of the state. People may be imprisoned, tortured, or killed. The remaining population becomes too afraid to resist the government 

### direction of popular discontent
Dictators frequently take people's concerns and unhappiness and turn it against groups that are blamed for causing the problem. Usually the targeted group is either a minority population in the nation or an outside force, such as another nation. By directing discontent, the dictator hopes all the hatred will be focused on the **scapegoat** and not on the government 

### controlled participation
even in dictatorships, people must feel a sense of participation in the political process. yet, dictators fear giving the people real political power. Often in dictatorships, elections are held even if there is no real choice. If there is a choice, ballot counting allows the government to manipulate the results. In other countries people are allowed to become members of the ruling party so they feel encouraged to work within the system
